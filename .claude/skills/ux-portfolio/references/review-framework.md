# Review Framework

How to evaluate an existing portfolio or case study against big tech hiring bars,
and how to present the findings. Read this before responding in Review mode.

Calibrate to Google, Meta, Apple, Amazon, Microsoft, and Netflix hiring bars, not
general UX industry standards. Every review ends with a leveling call: does this
read as Senior, or Lead/Staff? If Senior only, name the specific gap that caps it.

## 8-dimension evaluation framework

Score each dimension 1 to 5. Anchor scores to signals, not vibes. A 3 is "solid
Senior." A 5 is "clear Lead/Staff evidence a hiring committee could point to."

1. **Problem framing.** Is the problem real, sized, and owned, or handed down? Does
   the candidate show they chose what to work on and why it mattered to the business
   and the user? Senior: clearly states the problem. Lead: reframed or scoped the
   problem others accepted as given. Failure: jumps to solution with no problem.

2. **Role and attribution.** Can you tell exactly what this person did versus the
   team? "I" for solo work, "co-facilitated / partnered / coordinated" for shared
   work. Senior: clear role on their slice. Lead: drove work across teams and can
   prove it. Failure: "we" throughout, so the individual contribution is invisible.

3. **Process rigor.** Research, synthesis, iteration, validation, all shown with
   evidence rather than named. Senior: ran solid methods appropriate to the problem.
   Lead: chose methods under ambiguity and knew when to stop researching and ship.
   Failure: a method checklist ("I did interviews, personas, journey maps") with no
   insight attached.

4. **Design craft.** Visual execution, interaction detail, systems thinking, edge
   cases, accessibility. Senior: polished, consistent, considered states. Lead: work
   others reuse; set a bar for the surface. Failure: hero shots only, no states, no
   edge cases, no rationale for visual decisions.

5. **Decision-making under ambiguity.** Tradeoffs named, alternatives considered,
   constraints navigated. Senior: made good calls and can explain them. Lead: made
   calls with incomplete information and defended them against pushback. Failure: a
   tidy linear story where every decision was obvious in hindsight.

6. **Impact and outcomes.** Metrics with a source and timeframe, before to after,
   honest about attribution. Senior: shipped and can point to a result. Lead: moved
   a metric that mattered to the org, or shifted product direction. Failure: vanity
   metrics, unsourced numbers, or "the team was happy."

7. **Scope and influence.** The single strongest leveling signal. Did the work
   affect one feature, a product area, or the org and its practices? Senior: owned a
   feature end to end. Lead/Staff: defined scope, influenced roadmap beyond their
   team, created a framework or pattern others adopted, survived reorgs. Failure:
   strong execution confined to a single handed-down feature.

8. **Storytelling and communication.** Structure, hierarchy, concision, headers that
   carry meaning. Senior: clear and easy to follow. Lead: makes a reviewer feel the
   stakes and remember the outcome. Failure: wall of text, buried outcome, generic
   section labels, resume-bullet voice.

**Leveling call.** Scope (7) and influence gate the level more than craft. A
candidate can have 5s on craft and process and still read Senior if the work never
left their own feature. Many candidates have Lead-level work presented in a
Senior-level frame; distinguish "the work is Senior" from "the presentation is
Senior" and say which one you are looking at.

## Section header audit

Headers are the skim layer. A reviewer reads headers first and often only. Weak
headers are generic labels; strong headers follow an **Action + Outcome** pattern:
what the candidate did, and what happened because of it.

- Weak: "Research" — "The Challenge" — "Final Designs" — "Results"
- Strong: "Diary studies showed users abandoned at the upload step" — "Cutting the
  flow from 7 steps to 3 lifted completion 64 to 78 percent"

Audit steps: extract every header, read them top to bottom as a standalone story,
and check that (a) each header states a specific action or finding, (b) the sequence
alone tells the arc of the project, and (c) no header could be copied unchanged into
a different case study. Rewrite any header that fails. Do not invent an outcome to
fill the pattern; if no verified outcome exists, use an action-only header rather
than a fabricated result (see source verification in SKILL.md).

## Output structure

Present a review in this order:

1. **Leveling call up front.** One or two sentences: reads as Senior / Lead, and the
   single biggest reason. Lead with the verdict, not the preamble.
2. **Dimension scores.** The 8 dimensions with a one-line justification each. A
   compact table is fine here; this is analysis, not the portfolio itself.
3. **Top 3 fixes, prioritized by leverage.** The changes that would move the leveling
   call, most impactful first. Each fix is specific and actionable: what to change,
   where, and why it helps. "Move the outcome metrics above the fold on the checkout
   case study" — not "make it stronger."
4. **What exists vs what is missing.** Separate fixes the source material already
   supports from gaps that need new material. For gaps, ask rather than assume: "This
   would benefit from a peer quote on the cross-team work. Do you have one?"
5. **Header audit** when relevant, as a before/after list.

Never give feedback without specifics. Never recommend adding content that is not in
the source material. Flag the provenance of every recommendation.

## Consolidating multiple feedback sources

When reviewing several case studies or reconciling multiple reviewer inputs:

- Check title and role consistency across homepage, about page, case studies, and CV.
  Inconsistent leveling claims are an immediate credibility hit.
- Deduplicate feedback that recurs across pieces into one systemic note ("outcomes
  are buried in all three case studies") rather than repeating it per piece.
- When two inputs conflict, resolve by the hiring bar, not by splitting the
  difference, and say which you followed and why.
- Rank the consolidated list by leverage across the whole portfolio, so the candidate
  fixes the highest-impact issue once rather than chasing local notes.

## Request-specific adaptation

Match depth to the ask:

- **Quick gut check / "would you hire me"** — lead with the leveling call and the two
  or three things capping it. Skip the full dimension table unless asked.
- **Full audit** — all 8 dimensions, header audit, prioritized fixes, gap analysis.
- **"Is this Senior or Lead"** — center the scope and influence dimension, name the
  specific evidence that would move it up a level.
- **Header audit only** — go straight to the before/after header list.
- **Review then fix** — present the review first, let the user choose what to act on,
  then switch to Create mode. Never start editing before showing the review.
