Body copy and the big hero name. Weights 400 / 600 / 700. The face that says things.

This supersedes the brief's “Hanken = logo only” lock. That rule protected the wordmark's exclusivity, but Hanken 800 earns the heading role — a true grotesk with the Swiss lineage the system is built on. The concern is answered differently: the logo now differentiates by form, not by face — the m—k construction, the rule, the scroll collapse. A logo that is only distinctive because nothing else may use its font is borrowing its identity from a prohibition. No Hanken in body copy; no Inter in section titles.

The 12-column grid · 24px gutter · max 1440px

Every section answers to the grid — then breaks it deliberately (the full-bleed name, the oversized number). An unintentional break is just misalignment.

×

No shadows. There is no elevation scale. Separation comes from rules, space, and hairlines — not depth. If something needs to feel raised, it doesn't; give it space.

×

No radius scale. Binary only: pills are fully round (999px), cards and image boxes are square (0). Nothing in between.

×

No dark mode. Light reading surfaces are a medical requirement, not a preference. Do not author a dark override.

04 — Rules

The structural device · 2 weights · 1 ink

This system has no shadows and no rounded cards, so the rule does all the separating. That makes it structure, not decoration — which is why there are only two weights and one ink. Every specimen below is the live element, not a picture of one.

Section rule structural 1px · --color-text #14121a · 18.56:1

Sits above every band, with the title below it. The full-width ink line is the Swiss move: it declares a section without a heading having to shout. Paired with padding-top: 16px.

marissa klymkiw

The only rule with a fixed width — it's a glyph, not a divider. Same 2px weight as the masthead, which is what ties the mark to the hero. This is the element the logo's identity now rests on, so it doesn't get to drift.

Hairline separator 1px · --color-line #e6e4ea · 1.26:1

Type-specimen dividers, face cards, image-box borders. Separator only. At 1.26:1 it is deliberately near-invisible — it groups without announcing. See the contrast note below: this is legitimate here and illegitimate as an interactive boundary.

Hairline strong separator 1px · --color-line-strong #cfcbd6 · 1.59:1

The hover state of a hairline — a card border darkening on hover. Never text (1.59:1), and never the only thing identifying a control.

The contrast rule for rules. WCAG 1.4.11 (Non-text Contrast, AA) requires 3:1 for a boundary needed to identify an interactive component — an input's border, a button's outline, a tag's edge. It requires nothing of a purely decorative separator. So the two categories above are not a style distinction, they're a compliance one: structural rules and interactive boundaries owe 3:1; separators owe nothing. A hairline dividing two paragraphs at 1.26:1 is correct. The same hairline as the sole outline of a button is a defect — which is exactly why the tags are now ink.

07 — Voice

First person · plain · precise

Plain, precise, systems-minded. Short declaratives that land. Name things by what they do, not by what they’re called — “one front door for every device that can’t log in for itself,” not “Device Registration Portal.” Confident, builder-not-enabler. State the claim, then let evidence carry it. Rigor leads; warmth is texture.

The structure has to be right. So does the screen that sits on top of it.

08 — The seven principles

The system philosophy

01 Grid systems

Structure is visible and answerable. 12 columns, always.

02 Typography as the interface

Type isn’t dressing the content, it is the content.

03 White space by subtraction

Space is made by removing, not by padding.

04 Objectivity

The design serves the message, not the designer’s signature.

05 Reduction

Every element must justify existing.

06 Clarity

If it needs explaining, it failed.

07 Access is structural

Clear to everyone, or it isn’t clear. Contrast, focus, target size, and motion are load-bearing structure — not a compliance pass bolted on at the end.

Six are Swiss-canonical — the Müller-Brockmann lineage, formed in print. The seventh is the one print never had to answer. A poster has a single reader at a fixed size in fixed light; a web page has an unknown reader at an unknown size, zoomed, inverted, read aloud, or driven by keyboard. Objectivity that only holds for readers with unimpaired sight isn’t objectivity — it’s an assumption wearing objectivity’s clothes. So 07 isn’t an addendum to the six; it’s what 04 and 06 actually mean once the medium can’t assume its reader.

The floor is WCAG 2.2 AA (1.4.3 Contrast Minimum — 4.5:1 body, 3:1 large). Every color token in §01 ships with its measured ratio; if a choice can’t meet the floor, the choice is wrong, not the standard. Nothing is signaled by color alone. Every interactive element has a visible focus state. Note: WCAG Level A sets no contrast requirement at all — contrast enters at AA — so “Level A contrast” would be vacuously true and guarantee nothing. AA is the real floor.

The Swiss test, applied to every animation, gradient, icon, and flourish: does it aid understanding, or just prove a designer was here? If the latter, cut it.
