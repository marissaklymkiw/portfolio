sundae

A Memphis Group direction for the portfolio. Loud on purpose, deadpan in the copy, and it still passes AA.

Ettore Sottsass Milan 1981 Flat & keylined WCAG 2.2 AA
00 — The premise

The Swiss system asks what it can remove. This one asks what it can get away with.

Sottsass's argument was against the Modernist claim that ornament is dishonest. A laminate covered in confetti isn't lying about being laminate — it's just enjoying itself. Memphis is decoration promoted to structure.

So these two systems aren't a spectrum with a tasteful middle. Don't blend them. A Memphis page with Swiss restraint is a colorful page that failed. A Swiss page with a squiggle is a rigorous page that got vandalized.

Move 01

Flat blocks

No gradients, no blur, no depth-realism. Color arrives in slabs.

Move 02

Black keyline

An ink outline on everything filled. It's the grout — and it's the accessibility fix.

Move 03

A shape that does nothing

Not an icon. Doesn't indicate. There because it's fun. The load-bearing idea.

Move 04

Pattern as material

Terrazzo, stripes, dots, checkers — used where another system uses a surface color.

Move 05

Deliberate wrongness

A 3° tilt, a stray circle off the grid. Once or twice a screen. Never everywhere.

The budget

Three per viewport

Maximal, not infinite. Sottsass put loud objects on a plain floor.

01 — Color

Play hues are surfaces. Voice hues are text.

The governing rule

Every bright hue here fails as text on white — cyan is 2.16:1, yellow is 1.43:1. Those same hues carry ink type at 4.67–12.57:1. So bright color goes behind type, never is type. When a hue has to be text, use its deepened Voice sibling. No exception for "just a small label."

Play hues — fills, blocks, shapes. Never text.

Two easy mistakes

Pink clears AA body by 0.17. Legal, and tight. Headings, chips, short labels only — a paragraph on pink moves to Pink tint. Never lighten the ink to "soften" it.

Grape is inverted. The only hue that takes white type and the only one that must never take ink. Getting this backwards is the likeliest bug in the system.

Why the keyline isn't decoration

WCAG 1.4.11 wants 3:1 for a boundary that identifies a component. Against white: pink 3.85 ✓, grape 6.39 ✓, tomato 3.23 ✓ — but cyan 2.16 ✗, mint 1.80 ✗, yellow 1.43 ✗.

A yellow card floating on white has, in accessibility terms, no edge at all. The fix is already the aesthetic: a 2–3px ink keyline on every filled shape, chip, card, button and input. That coincidence is why this direction can be this loud and still pass — and why the outlines never get "cleaned up."

Correct

Yellow fill, ink keyline. The component has a boundary.

Wrong

Yellow fill, no keyline. 1.43:1 against white — the edge doesn't exist.

Non-negotiable

Body copy stays on a straight baseline at a sane measure. Tilt slabs, chips and images — never a paragraph. Rotated running text is a legibility failure wearing a costume, and it's especially punishing with astigmatism.

04 — Shapes & patterns

Things that mean nothing, specified rigorously.

These are components. They get the same rigor as a button — and all of them ship aria-hidden="true" and pointer-events:none, because a thing that means nothing must announce nothing.

Dot grid · safest ground

The decoration budget

Three per viewport. Past three, the shapes stop being jokes and become noise — and the page loses the white space that makes them land. Count before you add a fourth.

Never put text on terrazzo, checker, or stripes. A patterned ground destroys every ratio in section 01. Text goes on paper, bone, or a tint.

06 — Access

The part that doesn't get to be playful.

Principle 07 carries over from the Swiss system unchanged: clear to everyone, or it isn't clear. A maximal aesthetic raises the stakes rather than lowering the bar — there are simply more ways to fail.

RuleWhy
WCAG 2.2 AA floor, ratios computed not estimatedTightest pair is ink-on-pink at 4.67:1. Nothing else is under 5.
